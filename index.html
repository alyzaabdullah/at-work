Research

Tools that measure people without making them feel measured.

I'm a final-year Kinesiology student at McGill. I work where movement science, pain research and human-centered computing meet, and I build the tools I wish existed for the people I work with.

Movement. Motor difficulties show up across many conditions, from developmental coordination disorder to Parkinson's disease. They're usually measured with long, examiner-run assessments given months apart, and between those snapshots progress is guesswork. SYNC is my first attempt at filling that gap: short, play-based sessions that record the same measures the same way every time. I started with autistic children, the group my scoping review covers.

Pain. In the lab, I run behavioural experiments in translational models of long COVID and fibromyalgia pain. Pain is hard to measure in anyone who can't easily describe it: young children, people who communicate differently, people living with dementia. Tools built on self-report miss them. That is the direction I want to take in a PhD.

Learning. I also build learning tools. BLOOM teaches kids to judge AI answers rather than simply trust them, and Circle Log helps adults in a social-skills program keep practising between sessions.

Long term, I want to build assistive technology for people with neurodevelopmental and neurodegenerative conditions, and get it to everyone who needs it, not just the people who live near a research clinic.

Alyza Abdullah · sole author

Maps how motor difficulties in autistic children are assessed, which interventions are used, and where the evidence that they work runs thin. Its findings shaped SYNC's design.

Measurement tool · Aug 2026 – nowLive

SYNC

Designed and built solo

A tablet game that measures motor coordination. Kids tilt to guide a light onto a target in time with a soft beat, then hold it there. The child just plays; parents and therapists get the data.

The aim is a standardized measure that tracks motor skills continuously alongside intervention. Next steps are test-retest reliability and validation against established tests such as the MABC-2 and BOT-2.

Learning tool · 2026Live

BLOOM

Designed and built for HerWILL

AI literacy for learners aged 11 to 14. Learners commit to an answer before seeing the machine's, flag the tells of a broken answer, and judge an AI's plan.

A matched check-in and check-out measure whether they got better at telling sound answers from broken ones, rather than just more suspicious of everything.

Circle Log

Co-designed with program staff at Autisme sans limites

A practice log for a social-skills group. Participants note what they practised in about a minute. Facilitators walk into the next session knowing who practised, what everyone is working toward, and who wants a call partner.
